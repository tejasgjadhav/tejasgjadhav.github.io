The NAV pack that refuses to release itself

Price a constructed fund, compute NAV per share, and run three checks. A missing price holds the NAV. Nobody has to remember to look.

← All live labs

This page runs Python in your browser. Nothing is installed and nothing is sent anywhere. The engine is the same as labs/m08_nav_engine.py, with the price fetch swapped for a file that is already on this site.

What the lab does

Price the book, add the cash, take out the accruals, divide by the shares. Then check three things before anyone signs.

The arithmetic is the easy half. Multiply each holding's quantity by its price, add them up, add the cash, subtract the accrued expenses, and divide by the shares outstanding. That is NAV per share.

The exception list is the half that matters. A missing price means the number is incomplete, so the NAV is held rather than released. A price dated earlier than the fund's own pricing date is stale, and somebody has to re-query the vendor. A holding that moved more than 5% on the day is flagged for a human to confirm against the vendor, because a real move and a bad price look identical in a spreadsheet.

The status line is computed, not typed. That is the whole idea. A NAV pack that prints READY FOR REVIEW when a price is missing is worse than no pack at all.

This is a dated snapshot, not live data. The file behind this page is data/nav_prices.json, holding the closes for 18 September 2026. It carries five holdings with quantities, that day's close, the prior close and the price date, plus the cash balance, the accrued expense and the share count. The fund is a constructed example and is not a real fund. To run the same lab against live prices on your own machine:
pip3 install yfinance && python3 labs/m08_nav_engine.py  (add --inject for the faulted run)

What you should see

This page runs the course's fund on the course's prices, so it prints the course's numbers.

The clean run prints a NAV per share of 31.1720 and raises zero exceptions. That is the figure Module 8 quotes. Securities come to 2,871,402.00, cash adds 250,000.00, the accrued expense of 4,200.00 comes off, and 3,117,202.00 divided by 100,000 shares gives the NAV.

Tick Inject an error and the page reproduces the course's second run. It blanks the XOM price and backdates the MSFT price to 15 September, which is exactly what --inject does in the script. The NAV per share drops to 27.0835, two exceptions appear, and the status line flips to NAV STATUS: HELD.

Read that second number before you move on. It is wrong by four dollars a share, and the engine printed it anyway. A missing price contributes zero to the sum, so the arithmetic completes and looks finished. The exception list is the only thing standing between that number and a client statement.

One small difference from the course pack. The course's run fetched prices live and Yahoo returned more decimal places than it displays, so its market values carry a floating-point tail: 649,305.01 rather than 649,305.00. The snapshot on this site stores prices at two decimals, so this page prints the round cent. The chapter treats that tail as a lesson in its own right, and the fix is to round the price to the vendor's stated precision before multiplying.

Change the day-move limit to 1 and run it again. QQQ moved 0.63 percent and nothing fires, because no holding moved more than one percent that day. Drop it to 0.5 and QQQ and MSFT both appear. A threshold that never fires is not evidence of a clean book.
